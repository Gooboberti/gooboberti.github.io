Glossary

What you are doing

Each sector is an ordinary 9×9 sudoku. Tap the centre keystone to focus a sector, then place digits (works at mid-zoom too). Wrong guesses flash red and fade. Fill all 81 correctly and the sector crystallises.

Crystallising is the engine of everything else. The sector pays out, opens its neighbours, and spills its lumen over its own borders — driving anchors into the sectors around it, which weld those borders into a lumen network. That network is what carries growth outward. The Lumen network tab walks the whole chain.

Corruption never appears from nowhere, and it is not a stain. One ganglion goes bad and sends out a single tendril that sits on your maps and solves them before you can. A map it finishes is stolen for good. The only counter is your own growth line: run it into claimed ground and it strips the cells back, one at a time, for you to solve. Long straight runs of crystallised sectors pay escalating meridian bonuses — and wall the tendril out entirely.

What the digit colours mean

  • Pale white — a starting clue. It was carved into the puzzle for you and can never be changed.
  • Green, underlined — you placed it, and it was right. Correct placements lock permanently, which is why they cannot be erased.
  • Gold, dotted — an anchor: a free digit driven in by a neighbouring sector's lumen spill, or forced on you by resonance from the district above.
  • Cyan — a digit you have entered that the game has not confirmed. Red means it conflicts with a peer.
  • Lilac boxes — cells currently under a shield, safe from the rot.

Once a sector crystallises every digit in it settles to one calm colour, since the distinction no longer matters. A key to all of this sits in the corner of the screen while you are zoomed into a sector.

Sector states

  • SEALED — visible but not playable. Clear a neighbour to open it, or let the probe route through it: the probe is the only thing that breaks sealed ground now.
  • OPEN — playable right now.
  • CRYSTALLISED — solved. It conducts energy and feeds its keystone upward.
  • VOID — outside the creature. Nothing is written there and nothing is playable. It is not a puzzle you are missing.

If you get stuck

Use NOTES for pencil marks, AUTO to fill every candidate for 15 seconds (auto-refreshes while live), or REVEAL to buy one correct cell at random. Correct placements lock; when a digit’s last copy is found the pad key seals with fanfare. The strip above the number pad always tells you what the game expects next.

Keystones

The centre cell of every sector is its keystone, marked with a gold diamond. It is not just a cell — it is also one square of the puzzle one layer up.

The district

Take any 9×9 block of sectors. Their 81 keystones form a valid sudoku in their own right. That is a district. On the field, every 3×3 cluster is centred on a ganglion — cluster 1 wraps the heart, then each other ganglion, then the rings around them. Take a 9×9 block of districts and their keystones form a sudoku too. It goes up four layers.

Resonance

Because a district is a real sudoku, once eight keystones in one of its rows, columns or blocks are known, the ninth is forced. The game hands it to you — a solved cell in a sector you have never opened — and you will see light travel from the eight contributors into the forced one.

Deep resonance

A district's own keystone is a square in the region grid above it, so eight known district keystones force the ninth. That reaches hundreds of sectors away and then cascades back down inside the district it lands in.

Projection

Open the Keystone grid tab in the Atlas to play a cluster as a 3×3. Each cluster wraps a ganglion. Tap a tile, then a digit — a key with only one legal value locks itself from above. Tap again on a known tile to drop into that sector.

The creature is the board

A five-armed fractal organism is folded into the plane. Its limbs are thick near the heart at the origin and taper to filaments at the tips. Everything outside that silhouette is void. Zoom out to see the whole thing.

Difficulty follows anatomy

The arteries near the heart are generous with clues. Every branch you take thins them out, until the filaments are brutal: CALM → STABLE → TURBULENT → SEVERE → VOID.

Circulation and ganglia

Cleared tissue conducts. Light leaves the heart and travels as far as an unbroken chain of crystallised sectors allows. At every major fork sits a ganglion: the hardest puzzle around, worth triple, and it only ignites once circulation actually reaches it. Igniting one grants a permanent boon.

It is alive

  • Growth — clear an entire branch segment and the limb thickens, adding new playable tissue.
  • Spasms — a heavily circulated branch sometimes throws an unplanned filament out into the void.
  • Degeneration — distal tissue that circulation never reaches, and that you have never touched, starves: it sheds bonus digits and eventually re-seals. It never touches a sector you have written in.
  • Breathing — the pulse across the veins quickens as awakening rises.

The blight — and how to cut it

Corruption is not a stain. It is a rival solver, and it wants the same maps you do.

One ganglion goes bad. For about forty seconds the source swells — broken rings turning around a dark core, a gold countdown, no damage at all. Then it sends out a single tendril, and on every map that tendril reaches it sits down and starts filling the puzzle in, cell by cell, in its own crimson hand.

It solves, you stripThe rival fills a map in crimson. Your strand runs in and takes the cells back.

Stolen maps

Every cell it claims is written into the board in crimson and you cannot type over it. When it has claimed every non-clue cell on a map, that map is STOLEN — it will never crystallise for you and it pays you nothing.

  • The rot works at roughly one cell every few seconds, so a whole map takes it a long time. You can see the count on the plate: n / 81 CLAIMED, with a rim that fills as it works.
  • It takes open cells first. A cell you have already solved costs it about 2.6× as long to prise away. Racing it on a contested map genuinely slows it down.
  • A sector under a shield or secure cannot be claimed at all, and meridians still wall the tendril out entirely.
  • It only moves on to the next map once the one it is standing on is finished.

Stripping it back

You cannot touch the rot from a distance. There is no button, no bounty, no remote cauterise. The only thing in the game that affects it is your own growth line.

Where the probe's strand runs into claimed ground, two things happen at once. The tip that is actually standing on a crimson cell STRIPS it empty — it does not write over the rot, it burns the claim off and leaves the square for you. That costs extra charge, and a keystone costs more still. Meanwhile the whole map the strand has touched starts STRIPPING in the background: roughly one claimed tile every 1.25 seconds, handed back empty, with a small bounty. Circulation speeds it; the sector the head is sitting in strips fastest.

A corruption hole is different. That is a punched cell, not a rival digit. The probe MENDS it and writes the answer so the line can keep moving. If that hole is the keystone, the tip will say CAUTERISE — the centre has to be closed before the map can be annexed.

  • Linkage is permanent. Once the strand has been through a sector it keeps stripping it even after the head has moved on — so you can have several maps clearing at once behind a single lead head.
  • Strip a whole map and you get RECLAIMED: a bounty, integrity, and every cell back to solve yourself for full points. The tendril is also cut at that sector, orphaning everything beyond it.
  • Strip the source ganglion and the rot is finished outright — the single biggest bounty on the board. When the probe arrives there you will see ROOT CONTACT; keep the strand on that map until the last crimson cell is gone.

So the shape of a good campaign is: point the probe at the fouled ganglion, let the lead head drive toward it, and let the tail of the strand quietly hand you back every map the rival stole on the way.

Each arm grew under different rules and has its own colour, its own drone interval, and its own signature boon. Open the ❉ panel for the full dossier.

Echo — the same digit, a different sector

Place a digit, then place that same digit in a different sector within seven seconds, and the two placements link. A glowing arc arcs between the exact cells, the chain length shows at its crest, and the payout climbs steeply as you keep it going.

Echo chainSame digit, new sector, inside the window. The chain multiplies.

The payout is 6 × n × (n−1) lumen at chain length n, so it is worth nothing at 1, 12 at 2, 36 at 3, 72 at 4, and 264 by 8. It caps at a chain of 12. Repeating a digit inside the same sector does not extend the chain — the mechanic exists to pull you across the board, not to reward grinding one puzzle. The window resets the moment it lapses, so a chain is something you notice and chase, not something you can plan out in advance.

A practical way to use it: when you have found a digit that is forced in several nearby sectors, do not clear one sector then the next. Walk the digit across all of them first.

Spread — keeping sectors live

Every placement keeps its sector live for fifteen seconds, drawn with a breathing green rim that dims as the timer runs out. The chip on the right counts how many are live at once.

Live sectorsEach one you keep warm raises the multiplier on every bonus.

Two or more live sectors gives ×1.115 per extra sector, up to a ceiling of ×1.9 at nine. It multiplies your echo payouts and your line-close bursts, so the three systems compound: walking one digit across five sectors keeps all five live, which raises the multiplier, which raises the echo that walking it paid you in the first place.

It also has a defensive use. A live sector resists the rot: recent play slows the tendril crawling into it to a fraction of its speed.

Lattice integrity

A score that rewards the shape of your progress, not the amount. Completing a full row, column or block of keystones scores; resolving all 81 in a district scores far more; a region line more still. Every meridian you complete adds a further +0.07 to the multiplier, which now runs up to ×3.4. Integrity multiplies every lumen payout, passive income included, so sixty sectors cleared along one long line are worth several times sixty scattered ones.

Meridians as terrain

A run of nine or more is not only a payout. Its sectors are drawn with a gold spine, hold a long protective ward, and act as a permanent firebreak: the rot tendril cannot cross the line at all. Laying meridians deliberately around the tissue you care about is the cheapest corruption defence in the game — and the only one that pays you to build it.

Boons

Permanent upgrades from igniting ganglia: cheaper hints, cheaper breaches, a wider lumen spill, revealed keystones nearby, and richer lumen.

The chain, in one line

Solve a sector → it crystallises → it spills lumen over its borders → the spill drives anchors into the neighbours → those anchors weld the borders → welds carry lumen between keystone banks → banks pay for growth. Every step below is one link in that chain.

1 · SpillCrystallising floods lumen out across all four borders.
2 · AnchorsThe flood condenses into free digits along the neighbour's facing edge.
3 · WeldAnchors are contact points. More of them, more lumen the border carries.
4 · BanksKeystones store lumen and push it along the welds to the growing tip.

1 · The lumen spill

A sector holds the lumen its own digits produce. The moment it crystallises that structure locks and the sector can no longer hold it all — the surplus spills outward across every border it shares with an open neighbour. This is automatic and free; it is simply what finishing a sector does.

The spill is announced as LUMEN SPILL and you will see gold sparks land along the borders. It only reaches sectors that are open: a sealed or already-crystallised neighbour takes nothing.

2 · Anchors

Where the spill meets a neighbour it condenses along that neighbour's facing edge lane — the nine cells directly against the shared border — and freezes into anchors. An anchor is a correct digit, gold and dotted, given to you outright. How many you get per border is set by the SEEPAGE boon.

An anchor does three separate jobs, which is why it is worth more than a free digit:

  • It solves a cell. Nine anchors along a lane is a whole edge of a neighbouring puzzle handed to you.
  • It radiates a halo. Each anchor emits a soft lumen band down its own row and column, reaching past the sector edge into the sectors beyond. The halo pays a small continuous trickle and fades over roughly a minute, so it is a burst of proximity income, not a permanent one. Spend it while it is bright.
  • It welds the border. This part is permanent.

3 · What a weld actually is

A weld is a lumen conduit across the deadspace between two sectors. A border has nine facing cell pairs, one for each square along the seam. A pair becomes a contact when both of its cells hold a digit — and it does not matter at all who put them there. A spill anchor solves the far side for you; solving your own edge cell closes the pair from your side. Either way the contact counts.

This means ordinary solving welds the world together. Every digit you place on a sector edge is quietly widening the pipe to whatever is next door.

In numbers: a bare border carries about 3.5 lumen/min. Every closed contact adds +2.6, each crystallised sector on either end adds +7, each end sitting on a live vein adds +4, and corruption on either end halves the whole thing. Nine contacts between two crystallised sectors on the circulation is a genuinely fat pipe; one contact between two half-solved boards is a straw.

Tap the gap between any two sectors to open that border. You get the nine pairs as a row of pips, the full capacity breakdown line by line, whether the growth spine is currently pumping through it, and a plain-language note on what would improve it. While it is open the border lights up on the field: closed pairs draw solid gold, open pairs draw as a dashed pink lane, and every cell involved is ringed so you can see exactly which digits are missing.

Welds are what connected means in this game. Two sectors that share a welded border are one logistics unit: lumen banked in either of them can reach the other. Chain enough welds together and you have a supply line that can feed growth a long way from where the work was done.

On screen: every welded border keeps a faint permanent stitch across its gap — one tick per anchor, brighter when both sides are crystallised. Where the pulse spine runs over a weld it is drawn as a full pipe with a collar and a direction arrow.

Where probes come from

The lead probe is born in the heart itself — the core keystone of your starting ganglion — and it aims itself at whichever ganglion the rot has taken. You never have to launch it.

Every probe after that departs from one of the eight keystones surrounding the heart. A pad opens as soon as that sector is open, and each launch costs more than the last: 120, then 246, 504, 1034, and so on.

Open PROBE COMMAND from the Atlas to see the whole fleet. Pick a probe in the list, then pick a ganglion on the map to send it there — including rerouting the lead. Pick LAUNCH A NEW PROBE first to send a fresh one from the next free pad.

More than one probe

The probe you have been driving is the lead. Every ganglion you ignite can host one auxiliary probe of its own.

Two frontsThe lead cuts where you are working; an auxiliary walks its own route unattended.
  • To launch one, open PROBE ROUTE from the Atlas and pick a ganglion that is not the lead's target. It leaves from whichever ignited ganglion is closest to what you picked.
  • One per ganglion. An ignited ganglion that already has a probe out cannot send another until that one arrives.
  • They are slow and hands-off. An auxiliary annexes one keystone roughly every 11 seconds, a little faster in a rich world, and pays the same toll out of the same two stores. It cannot surge, because you are not solving beside it.
  • If the money runs out it waits rather than stalling for good, so an auxiliary will quietly eat your reserve if you leave several running.
  • On arrival it dissolves and the whole route it annexed stays yours.

How the probe actually runs

It aims itself at the fouled ganglion, starts on its own once your first map crystallises, and projects its whole route. Everything below happens along that route.

  • Sealed ground is its job. There is no manual breach any more — the probe opens sealed maps as it routes through them.
  • Solving drives it. Place a number in any map the strand runs through and that counts as a press on the tip: it pays and takes the next cell. Solving near the line gives a smaller shove.
  • Reaching a keystone costs a toll, paid automatically, priced by distance from your anchor ganglion.
  • Speed is your activity. Live sectors, a running echo chain, a chorus, local production and total income all multiply how fast it cuts.
  • Costs come from the reserve first, then from the network of keystone banks and welds behind it.

The probe

The probe is not a toggle any more, and it does not live in the tool nest. It sits on its own at the bottom left, to the right of VIEW, because it is the one tool that changes what the whole board is doing.

It will not run without a destination. Tapping it opens a map of the ganglia around you, drawn over the creature's own limbs, with the route to each one traced and each one labelled with how many sectors out it is and what percentage of that route is already solved. Pick one and the probe aims at it.

  • The route matters. Ground that is already solved is cheap for the probe to fissure through and raw ground is expensive, so a 60% route grows visibly faster than a 10% one even if it is longer. That number is the whole reason the map exists.
  • It fissures, it does not march. Between keystones the probe runs a cell-level search that hugs digits already in place, turning at whatever angle is cheapest. It reads as a crack finding its way through the board rather than a ruler line.
  • It pulses out and back. Energy shuttles from the nearest keystone bank to the growing tip and returns for more. While it is travelling, the tip waits.
  • The tip is a button. The violet square at the end of the chain shows the answer as a ghost and a collapsing ring. Tap it and you pay for that cell yourself, roughly one flip's worth, from the nearest bank first. It is the impatient option.
  • Reaching the ganglion ends the run. The probe goes idle and waits for you to choose where it goes next.

4 · Keystone banks

Every keystone the pulse claims becomes a node. A node banks the lumen its own sector produces — so where your production sits now matters, not just how much of it there is. Tap any keystone to open its bank readout. To see how each digit on the board pays into that bank, hold SIGHT beside the lumen ledger — the overlay only stays while you hold.

  • The ring around a keystone is its bank. It fills from local production and flares white when full.
  • A full bank pushes its surplus forward along the weld toward the growing tip. You will see the packet travel — and the weld's capacity caps how fast it can go.
  • A node with corruption in its own sector mends itself first, and holds part of its bank back from upstream pulls.
  • The growing tip pays for each new cell from its own bank, then pulls backward through the welds. Only when the whole spine is dry does it touch your global lumen.
  • The tip's fill level also sets how fast it grows, from ×0.45 on empty to ×1.30 on full. A starved tip crawls even when you are rich.
  • AUTO / PUSH / HOLD in the readout decide whether a node feeds the tip eagerly, normally, or not at all.

Hold SIGHT (beside the ledger) to see the digits paying into the bank, ringed and weighted, with each row and column’s rate printed in the margin. Release and the numbers leave.

5 · Reading the lines between sectors

Anything that crosses the deadspace between two sector plates is drawn across the gap on purpose, because a crossing is always worth more than a line inside one sector.

  • Faint ticks across a border — a welded border, one tick per closed contact pair. This is the permanent map of what is connected to what. Tap the gap to open it.
  • Pipe with a collar and an arrow — a weld valve carrying live traffic between two keystone banks. Thickness is capacity, brightness is current flow, the arrow is direction.
  • Bright gold weld with a diamond node — the lumen pulse conduit where the growth line itself has tied two sectors together.
  • Dashed violet bridge with brackets — a crossing the growth line intends to make but has not made yet. Turns pink when a gap is blocking it.
  • Glowing gold arc between ganglia — the probe’s network. Only the nodes, not every keystone in between.
  • Soft gold beam with an arrowhead — an anchor halo reaching out of its sector along that exact row or column. It fades; the weld underneath does not.
  • Wide flowing gold beam — a meridian: a straight run of crystallised sectors. Brightest where it crosses a boundary, and a wall the blight cannot pass.

Playing it well

  • Crystallise along the direction you want to grow, not in scattered pockets. Each finished sector welds the border ahead of it.
  • When you are choosing where to place next and it is a toss-up, take the edge cell. Interior digits pay; edge digits pay and widen a pipe.
  • Anchors are worth taking early. The halo income is on a fuse; the weld it leaves behind is not.
  • A tip that crawls is usually a weld problem, not a lumen problem. Open its readout and look at the anchor counts on its links.
  • Corruption anywhere on the supply line halves that weld. Mending a sector two steps back can speed up a tip that is nowhere near it.

Pause, saving and loading

  • The ❙❙ button beside the gear holds the whole lattice still. While paused nothing corrupts, no tendril crawls, no tissue starves and the clock on every shield stops.
  • The game already saves itself continuously. On top of that you get three save slots you control, and three autosaves that rotate on their own — every few minutes of play and after a sector crystallises, with slot 1 always the newest.
  • Each slot shows what is in it: sectors cleared, lumen, meridians laid, rot sources live, and how long ago it was written.
  • LOAD restores that point and restarts the page. Saving is automatic — the lattice is written continuously and the most recent autosave waits for you on the launch screen. EXPORT FILE writes it out as a .json to keep or carry to another device; IMPORT FILE reads one back in, from here or from the launch screen.

Straight stitch line keystone→keystone. Auto-walks the line; stops at gaps you must fill. Hands off at the next keystone; faster along a crystallised backbone.
Breach
Paying lumen to open a sealed sector next to your frontier.
Anchor
A free gold digit driven into a neighbour by a lumen spill. Solves a cell, radiates a fading halo, and permanently welds that border.
Halo
The soft row/column lumen band an anchor radiates. Pays a trickle and fades over about a minute.
Spill
The outward flood of lumen a sector releases the moment it crystallises. Only reaches open neighbours.
Weld
The lumen conduit across a shared border. Its capacity is set by how many anchors were driven into it.
Umbra
The rot's currency, and the mirror of lumen. It earns 1 per cell it buries (2 when it has to prise one off you) and 60 for a finished map, then spends it to open another front. The count is printed under the source.
Front
One map the rot is actively solving. It starts with one; each extra costs 55 × (n+1)^1.75 umbra and it will never run more than four.
Contact
One of the nine facing cell pairs on a border, closed when both its cells hold a digit — by anchor or by your own hand.
Bond
The closed-contact count on a border, 0 to 9. Shown per link in the keystone readout, and in full when you tap the border itself.
The Weaver
The fractal organism the whole board is built on.
Vein / tissue
Sectors that lie on the creature. Only these are playable.
Artery
The thickest, easiest tissue, near the heart.
Void
Everything outside the creature. Not a puzzle, not playable.
Heart
The origin sector, where circulation starts.
Circulation
Energy flowing from the heart through an unbroken chain of crystallised tissue.
Energised
Tag meaning circulation currently reaches this sector.
Ganglion
A node at a major fork. Hardest puzzles, triple pay, grants a boon when ignited.
Boon
A permanent upgrade from igniting a ganglion.
Awakening
Share of the whole creature cleared.
Growth
New tissue added when a branch segment is fully cleared.
Spasm
An unplanned new filament thrown out by a well-fed branch.
Degeneration
Untouched, unfed distal tissue slowly starving and re-sealing.
Cascade
Completing a row, column or box inside a sector. Pays a bonus that scales with your streak.
Exposure
The count of digits you have revealed. Pays lumen continuously, doubled where circulation reaches.
